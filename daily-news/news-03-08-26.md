## AI News Digest

### The Pentagon, Anthropic, and OpenAI: A Widening Rift

**Pentagon Blacklists Anthropic as "Supply-Chain Risk"**
The U.S. Department of Defense (DoD) has officially labeled AI firm Anthropic a supply-chain risk, a designation typically reserved for foreign adversaries. The action followed Anthropic's refusal to agree to terms that would allow the military to use its AI models for autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei announced the company will challenge the designation in court, while assuring most customers are unaffected. (Source: TechCrunch, Towards AI, Wired)

**OpenAI Accepts Pentagon Deal, Prompting Internal Dissent**
Hours after Anthropic's refusal became public, OpenAI signed a similar deal with the Pentagon, reversing its long-held ban on military applications. This move signals a major strategic shift for OpenAI and deepens the ethical divide among leading AI labs regarding military collaboration. In response, OpenAI's robotics lead, Caitlin Kalinowski, resigned from her role, citing the controversial agreement. (Source: TechCrunch, Towards AI)

**Cloud Providers Clarify Claude's Availability**
Microsoft, Google, and Amazon have confirmed that Anthropic's Claude models remain available to their non-defense cloud customers. The clarification was necessary to prevent commercial panic, assuring businesses that the DoD's designation does not impact their access to Claude via platforms like AWS Bedrock or Google Cloud. The statements separate the specific government contract dispute from the broader commercial availability of Anthropic's technology. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Report Alleges Prior DoD Use of OpenAI Models**
A report alleges the Pentagon was already experimenting with OpenAI's models for military purposes through Microsoft's Azure services before OpenAI officially lifted its ban on military use. This suggests the formal policy change may have been a move to legitimize existing or planned usage, rather than a completely new development. The testing highlights the complex relationships between AI labs, their cloud partners, and government agencies. (Source: Wired)

### Major Model and Platform Releases

**OpenAI Unveils GPT-5.4 with "Thinking" and "Pro" Versions**
OpenAI has launched GPT-5.4, its newest frontier model, described as its "most capable and efficient" model for professional work. The release continues the rapid pace of AI model advancement, offering specialized versions to cater to different user needs and complexities. The launch includes a standard "Pro" version and a more advanced "Thinking" version, aimed at complex reasoning tasks. (Source: TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**OpenAI and AWS Announce $110B Multi-Cloud Deal**
OpenAI has secured a massive multi-cloud deal with Amazon Web Services, valued at $110 billion, making AWS the exclusive third-party distributor for its upcoming "Frontier" agent platform. This architectural split gives AWS control over stateful, runtime agent environments via Bedrock, while Microsoft Azure retains exclusivity for stateless API calls. The deal expands an existing agreement and commits 2GW of AWS Trainium AI accelerator capacity to OpenAI. (Source: InfoQ)

**Microsoft Releases Compact Multimodal Model Phi-4-Reasoning-Vision-15B**
Microsoft has released Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B, a 15 billion-parameter open-weight multimodal model. This compact model is designed to balance high-quality reasoning with computational efficiency, targeting tasks in math, science, and GUI understanding. It excels at tasks requiring both image perception and selective reasoning, offering a powerful but smaller alternative to larger models. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Announces TensorFlow 2.21 and Production-Ready LiteRT**
Google has released TensorFlow 2.21, officially graduating LiteRT from preview to a production-ready framework for on-device inference. LiteRT will now serve as the universal on-device framework, replacing TensorFlow Lite (TFLite) and streamlining the deployment of ML models to edge and mobile devices. The update aims for faster GPU performance, new NPU acceleration, and easier deployment for PyTorch models on the edge. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Luma Launches Creative AI Agents with "Unified Intelligence" Models**
Luma has introduced Luma Agents, a new platform for generating end-to-end creative work, powered by its new "Unified Intelligence" models. This system is designed to coordinate multiple specialized AI systems to produce cohesive creative projects across text, images, video, and audio. The agents aim to move beyond single-task generation to orchestrate complex, multi-format creative workflows. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Google's February Updates Include Gemini 3.1 Pro**
Google announced its AI updates for February 2026, which included the release of Gemini 3.1 Pro and Nano Banana 2. These iterative updates demonstrate Google's continued effort to refine and expand its family of AI models to compete across different scales and applications. The announcement signals ongoing development in both high-end and on-device model capabilities. (Source: AI)

### New AI Tools and Enterprise Applications

**OpenAI Previews Codex Security for Automated Vulnerability Patching**
OpenAI has launched a research preview of Codex Security, an AI agent that analyzes codebases to detect, validate, and propose patches for security vulnerabilities. The tool aims to reduce the noise of traditional security scanners by using project context to identify and fix complex vulnerabilities with higher confidence. The preview is available to ChatGPT Enterprise, Business, and Edu customers. (Source: OpenAI News, MarkTechPost)

**AWS Launches Amazon Connect Health for Healthcare AI Agents**
Amazon Web Services has launched Amazon Connect Health, an AI agent platform specifically designed for the healthcare industry. The platform aims to automate and assist with common healthcare administrative tasks, freeing up staff and improving patient experience. Key functions include patient scheduling, clinical documentation, and patient identity verification. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Google Releases Android Bench to Evaluate LLMs for Developers**
Google has open-sourced Android Bench, a new evaluation framework and leaderboard for measuring LLM performance on Android development tasks. General coding benchmarks often miss the nuances of mobile development; this tool provides a specialized way to assess how well AI models can assist with Android-specific coding challenges. The dataset and test harness are publicly available on GitHub. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Rowspace Launches with $50M to Build AI for Private Equity**
A new startup, Rowspace, has launched with $50 million in funding from investors including Sequoia and Emergence to create an AI platform for the private equity industry. The platform aims to create an "institutional memory" by ingesting and analyzing decades of scattered deal memos and models to inform new investment decisions. The goal is to scale the expert judgment that is critical to private equity. (Source: AI News)

**Balyasny Asset Management Builds AI Research Engine with GPT-5.4**
Investment firm Balyasny Asset Management (BAM) has built a sophisticated AI research system using OpenAI's GPT-5.4 model. The system transforms investment analysis by using agentic workflows and rigorous model evaluation to process vast amounts of information at scale. This represents a high-end enterprise use case for the latest generation of frontier AI models in a complex financial domain. (Source: OpenAI News)

**Cursor Rolls Out "Automations" for Agentic Coding**
The AI-native code editor Cursor is introducing a new agentic coding system called Automations. This tool allows developers to automatically trigger AI agents to perform tasks based on events like a new code addition or a Slack message, moving beyond simple code completion. It represents a step towards more proactive and integrated AI assistance within the development environment. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Liquid AI Releases LocalCowork for Privacy-First Agent Workflows**
Liquid AI has released LocalCowork, an open-source desktop application for running AI agent workflows entirely on a local machine. By eliminating the need for API calls and data egress, LocalCowork provides a privacy-first solution for enterprises handling sensitive information. The system is powered by the new LFM2-24B-A2B model, which is optimized for low-latency, on-device tool use. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Descript Leverages OpenAI for Multilingual Video Dubbing**
Video editing software company Descript is using OpenAI models to power its multilingual video dubbing feature at scale. The AI optimizes translations for both semantic meaning and timing, allowing dubbed speech to sound natural and synchronized across different languages. This application showcases how AI can solve complex creative and logistical challenges in media production. (Source: OpenAI News)

### AI Research, Theory, and Development

**Yann LeCun Proposes "Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence" (SAI) to Replace AGI**
A new paper from Meta's Chief AI Scientist Yann LeCun and his team argues that the term "Artificial General Intelligence" (AGI) is ill-defined and inconsistent. The paper introduces a new concept, Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence (SAI), as a more concrete and measurable goal for the AI industry to pursue. This challenges the prevailing narrative in the race to build advanced AI. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google's Open-Source SpeciesNet Aids Wildlife Conservation**
Google has highlighted its open-source AI model, SpeciesNet, which is being used to help identify animal species from photos. The tool provides a practical application of AI for environmental and conservation efforts, helping researchers and organizations monitor wildlife populations. By open-sourcing the model, Google aims to make this technology widely accessible for conservation initiatives globally. (Source: AI)

**Research Questions Value of AGENTS.md Files in AI Coding**
A new research paper from ETH Zurich suggests that AGENTS.md files, a widely recommended practice for providing context to AI coding agents, may often hinder their performance. This finding contradicts common industry wisdom and could change how developers interact with and prompt AI coding assistants. The researchers recommend omitting LLM-generated context files and limiting human-written instructions. (Source: InfoQ)

## CRICKET News Digest

### T20 World Cup Final Set
**India Edge England in Thrilling Semi-Final**
Defending champions India secured their place in the T20 World Cup final by defeating England by a narrow seven-run margin. Despite a spectacular century from England's Jacob Bethell, India's total, anchored by Sanju Samson's 89, proved just enough, with Jasprit Bumrah's exceptional 18th over being a critical turning point. (Source: BBC Sport, ESPN Cricinfo.com)

**New Zealand and India Prepare for Title Showdown**
India will face New Zealand in the final, aiming to become the first team to successfully defend the T20 World Cup title, while the Kiwis are hunting for their first-ever World Cup trophy. New Zealand captain Mitchell Santner stated he "wouldn't mind breaking a few hearts," while India's Suryakumar Yadav urged his team to be courageous in the high-pressure match. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com, BBC Sport)

### Women's International Cricket
**Australia Demolishes India in One-Off Test**
Australia secured a dominant ten-wicket victory over India in the one-off Test at the WACA, concluding the match in just over two days. All-rounder Annabel Sutherland was the star with a sublime century (129), setting up a commanding first-innings lead and providing a fitting farewell for Alyssa Healy in her final Test. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com, BBC Sport)

**New Zealand Dominates Zimbabwe as Tahuhu Retires**
New Zealand clinched their ODI series against Zimbabwe, highlighted by a phenomenal performance from Amelia Kerr, who took seven wickets in the second match. The series also saw a concerning moment when Zimbabwe's Kelis Ndhlovu was hit by a bouncer and taken for scans, though she is reportedly recovering well. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

**Lea Tahuhu Calls Time on ODI Career**
New Zealand fast bowler Lea Tahuhu has announced her retirement from ODI cricket after a 15-year career. A key figure in the national side, she will continue to be available for T20 selection. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

### Domestic Cricket Highlights
**Historic Five-Wicket Haul in New Zealand First-Class Cricket**
Central Districts paceman Brett Randell made history by becoming the first bowler in 254 years of first-class cricket to take five wickets in five consecutive balls. The unprecedented spell occurred during a Plunket Shield match against Northern Districts. (Source: BBC Sport, ESPN Cricinfo.com)

**Sheffield Shield Final Picture Emerges**
South Australia are now clear favourites to reach the Sheffield Shield final against Victoria after strong performances from Travis Head and Nathan McAndrew. Meanwhile, rain forced a draw between Western Australia and Queensland, keeping Queensland's slim hopes alive, while Victoria officially ended Tasmania's chances. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

### England Cricket &amp; Administration
**McCullum's Future as England Coach Looks Secure**
Despite a difficult winter culminating in a T20 World Cup semi-final exit, England head coach Brendon McCullum and director of cricket Rob Key are expected to retain the support of the ECB. McCullum has reiterated his desire to continue in the role and defended his informal coaching methods. (Source: BBC Sport)

**Travel Disruptions Impact Multiple Teams and Tours**
The escalating conflict in West Asia has caused significant logistical challenges, forcing the England Women's training camp to be relocated from the UAE to South Africa. International teams like West Indies and South Africa required charter flights to depart India, while six English county clubs, including Lancashire and Somerset, have cancelled their pre-season tours to the Middle East. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com, BBC Sport)
