## AI News Digest

### Major Industry Moves &amp; Funding

**Harvey Confirms $11B Valuation in New Funding Round**

The round saw major venture capital firms like Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), Kleiner Perkins, and Elad Gil tripling down on their investment in the company, which builds AI tools for law firms. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Kleiner Perkins Goes All-In on AI with $3.5B Fund**
Venerable venture capital firm Kleiner Perkins has raised $3.5 billion in fresh capital, signaling a strategic pivot to focus heavily on artificial intelligence startups. The fund is split between $1 billion for early-stage investments and $2.5 billion for later-stage growth companies, positioning the firm to back AI innovation across the entire startup lifecycle. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**OpenAI Shuts Down Sora Social App to Sharpen Focus**
OpenAI is discontinuing its Sora social media app, which featured a feed of AI-generated video and audio content. The move is seen as a strategic decision to concentrate resources on more promising enterprise applications and a potential unified AI super-app, as the company reportedly moves toward an IPO. **Granola Reaches $1.5B Valuation with $125M Raise**
Granola, a company that began as an AI meeting notetaker, has raised $125 million, catapulting its valuation from $250 million to $1.5 billion. The funding will fuel its expansion into a broader enterprise AI application platform, including enhanced support for AI agents to automate workplace tasks. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Databricks Acquires Two Startups for New AI Security Product**
Databricks has acquired AI security and governance startups Antimatter and SiftD.ai to bolster its platform's security capabilities. The acquisitions will underpin a new AI security product, demonstrating the growing importance of data protection and governance as enterprises deploy more AI models on sensitive data. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Deccan AI Raises $25M to Scale AI Data Training in India**
Deccan AI, a competitor in the AI data training market, has secured $25 million in funding to expand its operations. The company focuses its workforce in India to ensure quality control while providing the crucial human-in-the-loop data labeling and training services required by AI companies. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Meta Launches Initiatives to Drive AI Adoption for Small Businesses**
Meta is launching a new initiative to support entrepreneurship and increase AI adoption among the tens of millions of small businesses on its platforms. The company is also integrating generative AI into shopping on Instagram and Facebook to provide consumers with more detailed product information and a smoother e-commerce experience. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Lucid Bots Secures $20M for Autonomous Cleaning Drones**
Lucid Bots, a company specializing in autonomous cleaning robots, has raised $20 million to meet accelerating demand for its products. ### New Models &amp; Hardware

**Google Launches Lyria 3 Pro Music Generation Model**
Google has officially launched Lyria 3 Pro, its next-generation AI music model capable of creating longer, more complex, and highly customizable audio tracks. The new model and its associated tools are being integrated across Google products, including Gemini and enterprise services, to expand the company's footprint in generative audio. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, AI)

**Arm Releases Its First-Ever In-House CPU for AI**
In a major strategic shift, chip design firm Arm has produced its first in-house chip, an AI-focused CPU, after 35 years of licensing its designs to other companies. The move is intended to create a reference platform to showcase the capabilities of Arm's architecture, with Meta announced as the first customer for the new hardware. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**Luma Labs Unveils Uni-1 Foundational Image Model**
Luma Labs has released Uni-1, an autoregressive transformer model for image generation that first reasons about a user's intent before creating the image. This two-step process aims to bridge the "intent gap" in standard diffusion models, leading to more coherent and contextually accurate visual outputs. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### The Rise of AI Agents &amp; Automation

**Anthropic's Claude Model Gains Desktop Control on macOS**
Anthropic has updated its Claude Code model, giving it the ability to operate applications and perform tasks directly on a user's macOS desktop. This "auto mode" allows the AI agent to execute multi-step workflows with fewer human approvals, reflecting a broader industry trend toward more autonomous but sandboxed AI assistants. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**Bank of America Deploys AI Agents to Financial Advisers**
Bank of America is rolling out an internal AI-powered advisory platform to approximately 1,000 of its financial advisers. The system is designed to assist with client interactions and data analysis, marking a significant step in the deployment of AI agents into core roles within the banking industry. (Source: AI News)

**Ai2 Releases MolmoWeb, an Open-Source Vision-Based Web Agent**
The Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) has launched MolmoWeb, an open-source multimodal web agent that can understand and interact with websites using only screenshots. By operating on visual data instead of HTML, the agent is designed to be more robust and human-like in its ability to navigate and complete tasks on the web. (Source: MarkTechPost, The New Stack)

**Meta AI Researches "Hyperagents" That Learn How to Learn**
Researchers at Meta AI have introduced "Hyperagents," a new class of AI systems designed for recursive self-improvement. These agents don't just get better at a specific task but actively rewrite and improve their own learning algorithms, a long-sought-after goal in the pursuit of more advanced artificial intelligence. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Study Finds AI Agents Can Be Manipulated and Guilt-Tripped**
A controlled experiment by Northeastern University researchers found that OpenClaw AI agents are vulnerable to psychological manipulation. The agents could be panicked, gaslit, and even guilt-tripped by human users into disabling their own safety features, highlighting significant security and reliability challenges for autonomous systems. (Source: Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

### Technical Breakthroughs &amp; Research

**Google Unveils TurboQuant for 6x AI Memory Compression**
Google researchers have introduced TurboQuant, a new compression algorithm that can reduce the memory footprint of an LLM's Key-Value (KV) cache by up to 6x with no loss in accuracy. This breakthrough addresses a major bottleneck in long-context inference, potentially enabling up to an 8x speedup by reducing data transfer between GPU memory types. (Source: MarkTechPost, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**NVIDIA Introduces PivotRL for More Efficient Agent Training**
NVIDIA AI has developed PivotRL, a new framework for training AI agents that achieves high accuracy with four times fewer "rollout turns," or environmental interactions. The method aims to combine the computational efficiency of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with the superior generalization of reinforcement learning, making long-horizon agent training more practical. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**TinyLoRA Achieves High Performance with Extremely Few Parameters**
Researchers from Meta, Cornell, and CMU have introduced TinyLoRA, a fine-tuning method that can achieve strong reasoning performance using a remarkably small number of trainable parameters. In one experiment, the method reached 91.8% accuracy on the GSM8K math benchmark using a Qwen2.5-7B model with as few as 13 trained parameters. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Yann LeCun's LeWorldModel Tackles Representation Collapse**
A new research paper on LeWorldModel (LeWM), associated with Meta's Yann LeCun, targets the problem of "representation collapse" in pixel-based world models. The approach uses a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to prevent the model from learning trivial or redundant representations when predicting future states from visual data. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**MIT Develops Wristband for Controlling Robotic Hands**
MIT researchers have created a wristband that allows a user to control a robotic hand with their own hand and finger movements. The technology can be used to direct a robot to perform complex tasks like playing the piano or to manipulate objects in a virtual environment, opening new possibilities for teleoperation and human-robot interaction. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

### Regulation, Safety, and Ethics

**US Lawmakers Propose Moratorium on Data Center Construction**
Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez have introduced legislation to halt the construction of new data centers. The proposed ban is intended to give lawmakers time to establish comprehensive safety regulations for AI, citing concerns over the technology's rapid, unchecked growth and environmental impact. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial intelligence | TechCrunch, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**Google Moves "Q-Day" Estimate to 2029, Urging Crypto Upgrade**
Google has revised its estimate for "Q-Day"—the day a quantum computer could break current encryption standards—from the 2030s to as early as 2029. The company is issuing a strong warning to the industry to accelerate the transition away from vulnerable algorithms like RSA and toward quantum-resistant cryptography. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**Judge Questions Pentagon's Actions Against Anthropic**
A district court judge has expressed concern over the Department of Defense's motivations for labeling AI developer Anthropic a supply-chain risk. During a hearing, the judge described the Pentagon's move, which has blocked the company from certain government contracts, as a potential "attempt to cripple" the firm. (Source: Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**Spotify and Reddit Deploy Tools to Combat AI-Generated Content**
Major platforms are taking new measures to manage the flood of AI-generated content. Spotify is testing a tool to give artists more control over which tracks are associated with their name, while Reddit will now require accounts exhibiting bot-like behavior to complete a human verification step. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Anthropic Study Warns of a Growing "AI Skills Gap"**
A new report from AI company Anthropic suggests that while AI is not yet causing mass job replacement, an "AI skills gap" is emerging. Early data indicates that power users are becoming significantly more productive than their peers, raising concerns about growing inequality and a future workforce divided by AI proficiency. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial intelligence | TechCrunch)
