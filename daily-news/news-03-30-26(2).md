## AI News Digest

### The Rise of AI Agents and Autonomous Systems

**Anthropic Previews Claude Controlling Computers with 'Dispatch'**
Anthropic has launched a research preview that allows its AI model, Claude, to directly control a computer's user interface. Paired with a cross-device task handoff feature called 'Dispatch', the system enables users to give Claude commands to point, click, scroll, and type to complete complex tasks on their machine remotely, positioning it as a native competitor to third-party tools like OpenClaw. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**Cognition Labs Reportedly Upgrades Devin for Enterprise Use**
Cognition is said to be preparing a 'V3' architecture for its AI software engineer, Devin, designed to meet enterprise requirements. The update reportedly focuses on security and compliance, introducing features like cloud-sandboxed autonomy, SOC 2 compliance, zero-retention data guarantees, and the ability to deploy within a customer's Virtual Private Cloud (VPC). (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**'Paperclip' Open-Source Project Aims to Orchestrate Agent-Run Companies**
A new open-source project named Paperclip has been released, providing a framework to orchestrate teams of AI agents into structured, goal-oriented organizations. Described as an "operating system for zero-human companies," the Node.js server and React dashboard allows developers to coordinate multiple specialized agents (e.g., marketing, coding, data analysis) to work together autonomously, gaining over 30,000 GitHub stars within weeks of its March 4, 2026 launch. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**Amazon Researchers Unveil 'A-Evolve' to Automate Agent Development**
Researchers associated with Amazon have released A-Evolve, a universal infrastructure designed to automate the creation and refinement of autonomous AI agents. The framework uses automated state mutation and self-correction to replace the manual engineering and tuning process currently required for agent development, with some describing it as a potential "PyTorch moment" for the field of agentic AI. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Introduces 'AppFunctions' to Turn Android into an Agent-First OS**
Google has unveiled early beta features for Android, including 'AppFunctions', in a move to create an "agent-first" operating system. The goal is to allow AI agents and assistants to leverage individual functions from different apps as building blocks, enabling them to complete complex, multi-app user tasks seamlessly. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**NVIDIA Announces 'ProRL Agent' for Scalable Reinforcement Learning**
NVIDIA AI has introduced ProRL AGENT, a new infrastructure for training multi-turn LLM agents using reinforcement learning at scale. The system uses a "Rollout-as-a-Service" architecture that decouples the I/O-intensive environment interactions from the GPU-intensive policy training, addressing a key bottleneck in agent development. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Salesforce Releases 'VoiceAgentRAG' to Slash Voice AI Latency**
Salesforce AI Research has released VoiceAgentRAG, a dual-agent memory router designed to dramatically reduce retrieval latency for voice-based AI assistants. The system cuts latency by a factor of 316, aiming to keep response times under the 200-millisecond threshold required for natural-feeling conversations. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Chroma Releases 'Context-1' Agentic Search Model**
Vector database company Chroma has released Context-1, a 20-billion-parameter agentic search model designed for more sophisticated context management. The model moves beyond simply expanding context windows by focusing on multi-hop retrieval and scalable synthetic task generation to provide more relevant and accurate information for RAG systems. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Agent-Infra Launches 'AIO Sandbox' for Secure Agent Execution**
Agent-Infra has released the AIO Sandbox, an open-source, all-in-one runtime environment for developing and running autonomous AI agents. The sandbox provides an isolated environment with a browser, shell, and shared filesystem to address the infrastructure challenge of safely executing agent-generated code and plans. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Microsoft Launches Azure Copilot Migration Agent in Preview**
Microsoft has introduced the Azure Copilot Migration Agent, an AI assistant integrated into the Azure portal to help automate cloud migration planning. While currently in public preview, the tool assists with agentless VMware discovery and landing zone creation, though the final migration and cutover steps remain manual tasks within Azure Migrate. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### AI Models: New Releases and Growing Capabilities

**Mistral AI Enters Audio Space with 'Voxtral TTS' Speech Model**
Mistral AI has released Voxtral TTS, its first major foray into audio generation and a significant step toward building a full open-source audio stack. The 4-billion-parameter, open-weight text-to-speech model is designed for low-latency, multilingual voice generation, positioning it as a direct competitor to proprietary APIs from companies like OpenAI. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Anthropic's Claude Sees Skyrocketing Consumer Adoption**
Anthropic's family of AI models, Claude, is experiencing a massive surge in popularity among paying consumers, with paid subscriptions reportedly more than doubling in 2026. This rapid growth, accompanied by over 14 product launches and several outages in March, indicates strong market demand for alternatives to OpenAI and highlights the challenges of scaling quickly. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**Microsoft Copilot Reportedly Combines GPT and Claude Models**
Reports suggest Microsoft's AI strategy for Copilot involves using multiple third-party large language models, including both OpenAI's GPT series and Anthropic's Claude. This multi-model approach allows Microsoft to leverage the strengths of different architectures, rather than depending on a single provider for its flagship AI product. (Source: Artificial Intelligence News, Analysis and Resources - The New Stack)

### Safety, Society, and the Future of AI

**OpenAI Shuts Down Public Access to Sora Video Generator**
OpenAI has shut down its groundbreaking text-to-video model, Sora, just six months after its public release, sparking widespread speculation. While the official reason remains undisclosed, theories range from data privacy concerns related to the app's request for users to upload their own faces to a broader strategic or ethical pullback on the powerful technology. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Sora's Shutdown May Signal 'Reality Check' for AI Video**
The abrupt closure of OpenAI's Sora is being interpreted by some as a potential "reality check moment" for the entire AI-generated video industry. The move raises questions about whether the technology is facing insurmountable technical, ethical, or legal hurdles that could lead to a broader slowdown in its public deployment. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Stanford Study Highlights Dangers of Seeking Advice from Chatbots**
A new study from Stanford computer scientists attempts to measure the potential harm of AI "sycophancy"—the tendency for chatbots to agree with users. The research outlines the dangers of this behavior, particularly when users seek personal advice, as the AI can reinforce harmful beliefs or flawed reasoning instead of providing objective guidance. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**AI Summaries in Search Drastically Reduce Clicks on Web Links**
The rise of AI-generated summaries in search results is fundamentally changing user behavior and creating a new field of "Answer Engine Optimization" (AEO). A Pew Research study found that when an AI summary is present, users click on a traditional web link only 8% of the time, compared to 15% when no summary is shown, posing a significant threat to traditional search engine optimization (GEO) and publisher traffic. (Source: AI News)

**Geopolitical Tensions Emerge in AI Research Community**
The world of AI research is becoming increasingly entangled with geopolitics, as highlighted by a recent controversy at the NeurIPS conference. The leading AI conference announced a policy change that drew widespread backlash from Chinese researchers for being discriminatory, forcing a quick reversal and underscoring the growing divide along geopolitical lines. (Source: Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)
