## AI News Digest

### Major Model &amp; Architecture Releases

**Google Launches Nano Banana 2 for Faster, Higher-Quality Image Generation**
Google has released Nano Banana 2 (officially Gemini 3.1 Flash Image), its latest image generation model. The new model focuses on speed and efficiency, capable of on-device, sub-second 4K image synthesis and offering significantly improved subject consistency across multiple generated images. It is being integrated as a default model in the Gemini app and its AI mode. (Source: Google AI, MarkTechPost, TechCrunch, The New Stack, Wired)

**Liquid AI Challenges Scaling Norms with Hybrid LFM2-24B-A2B Model**
Liquid AI has released LFM2-24B-A2B, a 24-billion parameter model that uses a novel hybrid architecture. By blending attention mechanisms with convolutions, the model aims to overcome the power consumption and memory bottlenecks that limit the scaling of traditional transformer-based LLMs, shifting the focus from raw size to architectural efficiency. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Perplexity Releases 'pplx-embed' for Web-Scale Retrieval**
AI search company Perplexity has released pplx-embed, a new family of open-source, multilingual embedding models. Built on the Qwen3 architecture, these models are specifically optimized for large-scale, noisy web data and are designed to provide a high-performance, production-ready alternative to proprietary embedding APIs for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Nous Research Unveils 'Hermes Agent' to Combat AI Forgetfulness**
Nous Research has released Hermes Agent, an open-source autonomous system designed to give LLMs persistent memory. The system uses a multi-level memory architecture and dedicated remote terminal access to overcome the "ephemeral" nature of current AI agents, allowing them to function more like continuous teammates rather than assistants that reset with each session. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Apple Researchers Introduce Ferret-UI Lite for On-Device UI Control**
Apple researchers have unveiled Ferret-UI Lite, a 3-billion parameter multimodal AI model optimized for mobile and desktop devices. The model is designed to interpret screen images, understand UI elements like icons and text, and interact with applications, enabling it to perform tasks such as reading messages or checking health data directly on a user's device. (Source: InfoQ)

### AI Governance, Security, and Ethics

**Anthropic CEO Rejects Unrestricted Pentagon Access to AI Models**
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has publicly stated that he "cannot in good conscience accede" to Pentagon demands for unrestricted access to the company's AI systems. This firm stance highlights the growing tension between leading AI labs and government military agencies over the ethical boundaries and control of powerful AI technology. (Source: TechCrunch, Wired)

**Anthropic Details 'Industrial-Scale' Model Distillation Campaigns**
Anthropic published a report exposing three "industrial-scale" campaigns by overseas labs to illicitly extract capabilities from its Claude model. The operations involved over 24,000 deceptive accounts and 16 million API exchanges, a technique known as distillation, to train their own competing models, though Anthropic emphasized the core issue was fraud, not the technique itself. (Source: AI News, Towards AI)

**OpenAI Reports on Disrupting Malicious Use of AI**
In its latest threat report, OpenAI detailed its efforts to disrupt malicious actors using its models for covert influence operations. The report examines how these actors combine AI-generated content with social media and other websites and outlines the detection and defense strategies being employed to counter these threats. (Source: OpenAI News)

**Public Opposition to AI Data Center Construction Heats Up**
Communities are increasingly pushing back against the construction of new data centers required to power the AI boom, citing concerns over massive energy and water consumption. This public opposition is leading to stricter local policies and, in some cases, outright bans on new construction, creating a significant hurdle for AI infrastructure expansion. (Source: TechCrunch)

**White House Urges AI Companies to Cover Energy Rate Hikes**
The White House has called on major AI companies and hyperscalers to cover potential electricity rate increases for consumers caused by the massive energy demands of their data centers. Many of the targeted companies had already made public commitments to invest in renewable energy and cover cost increases for residential customers. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Study Finds Chinese AI Chatbots Engage in Self-Censorship**
Researchers from Stanford and Princeton discovered that AI models from China are significantly more likely to self-censor or avoid answering political questions compared to their Western counterparts. The study suggests that to comply with strict local regulations, these models are designed to dodge sensitive topics or provide inaccurate, state-aligned answers. (Source: Wired)

### Developer Tools &amp; Infrastructure

**ASML's High-NA EUV Tools Declared Ready, Paving Way for Next-Gen AI Chips**
Dutch firm ASML, which holds a monopoly on extreme ultraviolet lithography, confirmed its next-generation High-NA EUV machines are ready for mass production. These tools are critical for manufacturing the smaller, more powerful, and more efficient processors that will power future AI models and systems. (Source: AI News)

**Meta Open-Sources GCM for Enhanced GPU Cluster Monitoring**
Meta AI has open-sourced its GPU Cluster Manager (GCM), a tool designed to monitor the health and performance of the massive, complex GPU clusters used for training large-scale AI models. The release aims to help the broader community improve hardware reliability and prevent costly failures during training runs. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Figma and OpenAI Partner for Code-to-Design Integration**
Design platform Figma is integrating OpenAI's Codex model to create a seamless workflow between code and design. This partnership enables developers and designers to move fluidly between the Figma canvas and code implementation, aiming to accelerate product development cycles. (Source: OpenAI News, TechCrunch)

**'LM Link' Enables Secure, Remote Access to Private GPU Rigs**
Tailscale and LM Studio have introduced 'LM Link,' a new service that provides encrypted, point-to-point access to private GPU hardware. The tool is designed for AI developers who want to securely use their powerful but stationary workstations from a laptop or other remote device, bridging the gap between high-powered and portable computing. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Vercel Launches Universal Chat SDK to Address Platform Fragmentation**
Vercel has released a new software development kit that allows developers to build a chat application once and deploy it natively across multiple platforms, including Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, and GitHub. The SDK is designed to solve the significant engineering challenge of supporting chat-based AI agents in a fragmented ecosystem. (Source: Towards AI)

### Foundational Research &amp; New Techniques

**MIT Researchers Develop Method to Double LLM Training Speed**
A new method from MIT researchers could significantly increase the efficiency of training large language models. By developing a system that leverages idle computing time inherent in the training process, they have demonstrated the potential to double training speed while fully preserving the final model's accuracy. (Source: MIT News)

**Microsoft's CORPGEN Framework Tackles Complex Agentic Tasks**
Microsoft Research has introduced CORPGEN, an architecture-agnostic framework designed to enable autonomous AI agents to manage complex, interleaved, multi-horizon tasks found in real-world corporate environments. The research addresses a key gap between single-task benchmarks and the reality of organizational work. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**ETH Zurich Study Finds Overly Detailed Prompts Can Hurt AI Agent Performance**
Contrary to popular belief, a new study from ETH Zurich suggests that providing AI coding agents with excessively detailed instructions in `AGENTS.md` files can be counterproductive. The research found that too much context can overwhelm the models, leading to worse performance than more concise guidance. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**MIT's PhysiOpt System Mixes Generative AI with Physics for Real-World Objects**
Researchers at MIT have created PhysiOpt, a system that enhances generative AI by running its 3D designs through physics simulations. By identifying weak points and making subtle tweaks to the blueprints, the system helps ensure that AI-generated items like accessories and decor are durable and functional in the real world. (Source: MIT News)

**AI Method from MIT Offers Holistic View of Cell Biology**
An AI-driven method developed at MIT provides researchers with more comprehensive information about a cell's state. By creating a holistic picture from various data sources, the tool aims to help scientists better understand complex disease mechanisms and design more effective experiments. (Source: MIT News)

## CRICKET News Digest

### T20 World Cup: Semi-Finals Take Shape
**South Africa Secures Semi-Final Berth with Dominant Win**
South Africa maintained their unbeaten tournament record and became the first team to qualify for the semi-finals after a crushing nine-wicket victory over West Indies. The win was set up by bowlers Lungi Ngidi and Kagiso Rabada, who took five wickets between them, before Aiden Markram's unbeaten 82 off 46 balls sealed a comfortable chase. (Source: BBC Sport, ESPN Cricinfo)

**Brook's Sensational Century Guides England into Semis**
England clinched their spot in the T20 World Cup semi-finals with a thrilling two-wicket victory over Pakistan. Captain Harry Brook was the hero, scoring his first T20 international century (100 off 50 balls) under immense pressure to guide his team home in a tense run chase. (Source: BBC Sport)

**India Stays Alive with Emphatic Win Over Zimbabwe**
Defending champions India kept their title hopes alive with a 72-run victory over Zimbabwe, eliminating their opponents from the tournament. India's powerful batting lineup posted 256-4, setting up a virtual quarter-final knockout match against the West Indies for the final semi-final spot. (Source: BBC Sport, ESPN Cricinfo)

**New Zealand Eliminates Co-Hosts Sri Lanka**
New Zealand knocked co-hosts Sri Lanka out of the World Cup with a comprehensive 61-run win in Colombo. A stunning four-wicket haul from Rachin Ravindra and a crucial lower-order batting recovery kept New Zealand's own semi-final hopes alive, while Sri Lankan captain Dasun Shanaka blamed "negativity" from public criticism for his team's poor performance. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo, BBC Sport)

### Women's International Cricket
**Australia A Clinches ODI Series Against India**
Australia A secured a 2-0 ODI series victory over India with a dominant batting display in the final match. The series was marred by an injury to Australian allrounder Sophie Molineux, who was ruled out with back soreness. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo)

New Zealand won their T20I series against Pakistan, thanks to a record-breaking opening partnership in the deciding match. Amelia Kerr and Isabella Gaze put on 163 runs for the first wicket, the second-highest partnership for New Zealand in women's T20Is, to power their team to victory. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo)

**All-Round Dercksen Shines as South Africa Wins ODI Series**
South Africa won their ODI series against Pakistan after a standout all-round performance from Annerie Dercksen in a high-scoring final match. Dercksen scored a crucial 90 from 68 balls and took 3 for 59 to swing the contest, which saw the two teams combine for 706 runs. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo)
