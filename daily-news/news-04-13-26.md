### New AI Models &amp; Hardware

**NVIDIA Accelerates Roadmap with "Vera Rubin" AI Platform**
NVIDIA announced its next-generation AI platform, codenamed "Vera Rubin," which is scheduled to ship to customers in the second half of 2026, following closely on the heels of its Blackwell architecture. The new platform represents a major leap, moving to a 3nm process, incorporating HBM4 memory, and introducing a specialized "Rubin CPX" GPU designed specifically for long-context inference. The Rubin GPU will feature 336 billion transistors and deliver 50 PFLOPS of FP4 inference performance. (Source: Towards AI)

**MiniMax Open-Sources Self-Evolving Agent Model M2.7**
AI research firm MiniMax has publicly released the model weights for MiniMax M2.7, its most capable open-source model. This model is significant for being the first from the company to actively participate in its own development cycle, a concept described as "self-evolving." It has demonstrated strong performance on agentic benchmarks, scoring 56.22% on SWE-Pro and 57.0% on Terminal Bench 2. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Liquid AI Releases LFM2.5-VL-450M for Edge Devices**
Liquid AI has launched LFM2.5-VL-450M, a highly efficient 450-million-parameter vision-language model designed for edge computing. The model brings sophisticated features like bounding box prediction, function calling, and multilingual support to low-power hardware such as NVIDIA Jetson Orin. It is capable of running with sub-250ms inference times, enabling more powerful AI applications to run directly on devices. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Kepler Opens Largest Orbital Compute Cluster for Business**
Kepler Communications has launched commercial access to what it calls the largest compute cluster in Earth orbit, consisting of 40 GPUs. This "in-space cloud" opens up new possibilities for real-time satellite data processing and orbital AI applications. The company announced that Sophia Space is one of its newest customers for the service. (Source: TechCrunch)

### AI Research and Techniques

**Meta and KAUST Researchers Propose "Neural Computers"**
A new research paper from Meta AI and King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) introduces the theoretical concept of "Neural Computers." This radical architectural proposal suggests that a single, learned neural network could function as a complete computer, folding computation, memory, and I/O into one unified model, potentially moving beyond the limitations of traditional hardware design. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**TriAttention Technique Promises 2.5x Higher LLM Throughput**
Researchers from MIT, NVIDIA, and Zhejiang University have proposed TriAttention, a novel compression method for the KV cache in large language models. The technique aims to solve a major inference bottleneck by compressing the data stored during long reasoning tasks, claiming to match the performance of full attention while increasing throughput by a factor of 2.5. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Building a "Hallucination-Free" Multi-Agent AI for Legal Research**
A developer has detailed a "zero-trust" architecture for a multi-agent legal AI system specifically designed to prevent factual inaccuracies, or "hallucinations." The system uses techniques like tool-mandatory verification and adversarial prompting to force the AI to cross-reference information with verified legal databases and to state "I don't know" when it cannot find a reliable answer, a crucial capability for high-stakes professional domains. (Source: Towards AI)

**Understanding "Test-Time Compute" in Advanced AI Models**
An analysis of models like DeepSeek-R1 and Anthropic's Claude explains the mechanism of "test-time compute," where a model dynamically allocates more computational power during inference to solve a difficult problem. This allows the model to "think" for longer on a complex query without requiring a larger parameter count, representing a key architectural shift behind recent gains in AI reasoning abilities. (Source: Towards AI)

**How Knowledge Distillation Creates Efficient Production Models**
Knowledge Distillation is a technique used to compress the intelligence of a large, complex "teacher" model or an ensemble of models into a smaller, more efficient "student" model. This process allows businesses to benefit from the high accuracy of massive research models while deploying smaller, faster, and cheaper versions in production environments where latency and cost are primary concerns. (Source: MarkTechPost)
