## AI News Digest

### AI Models and Frameworks

**Google Releases Cost-Effective Video Generation Model Veo 3.1 Lite**
Google has launched Veo 3.1 Lite, a more affordable and faster tier in its generative video portfolio, now accessible through the Gemini API. The model is designed to lower the cost-per-second of generated content, addressing a major barrier for developers looking to deploy video generation at scale. This move aims to make high-quality video generation more accessible for a wider range of applications. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Alibaba Launches Qwen3.5 Omni, a Native Multimodal Model**
The Alibaba Qwen team has released Qwen3.5-Omni, a natively omnimodal model designed to process text, audio, and video, and engage in real-time interaction. Positioned as a direct competitor to models like Gemini 3.1 Pro, it represents a shift from "wrapper" models to end-to-end integrated architectures that can handle multiple data types seamlessly. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Microsoft Releases Harrier-OSS-v1 Multilingual Embedding Models**
Microsoft AI has introduced Harrier-OSS-v1, a new family of three open-source multilingual text embedding models. The models, available in 270M, 0.6B, and 27B parameter sizes, have achieved state-of-the-art results on the Multilingual MTEB v2 benchmark. Their purpose is to provide high-quality semantic representations across a broad spectrum of languages for various AI tasks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**IBM and TII Launch New Compact Multimodal Models**
Two new compact multimodal models have been released: IBM's Granite 4.0 3B Vision and TII's Falcon Perception. Granite 4.0 is a 3-billion-parameter model optimized for understanding enterprise documents, while Falcon Perception is a new vision-language model from the creators of the Falcon LLM series. Both models are available on Hugging Face and target efficient, specialized multimodal applications. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

**Liquid AI Challenges Scaling Laws with Compact LFM2.5-350M Model**
Liquid AI has released LFM2.5-350M, a compact 350-million-parameter model that demonstrates high performance despite its small size. The model was trained on an extensive 28 trillion tokens and refined with large-scale reinforcement learning, challenging the conventional wisdom that more parameters are always required for greater intelligence. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Meta Research Explores Self-Improving AI and Brain Activity Prediction**
Meta has unveiled research on two fronts: "HyperAgents," an AI that can rewrite its own learning rules to improve, and TRIBE v2, a trimodal AI model that can predict human brain activity. TRIBE v2 was trained on 1,115 hours of brain scans from 720 subjects, allowing it to simulate complex neuroscience experiments without live subjects. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

### Developer Tools and Infrastructure

**Hugging Face Releases TRL v1.0 for Production-Ready Model Training**
Hugging Face has launched version 1.0 of its TRL (Transformer Reinforcement Learning) library, transitioning it from a research tool to a stable, production-ready framework. TRL v1.0 provides a unified API for the entire post-training pipeline, including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reward Modeling, and alignment techniques like DPO, standardizing the process for developers. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**A-Evolve Framework Aims to Automate AI Agent Development**
Researchers have released A-Evolve, a new framework designed to automate the creation and improvement of autonomous AI agents. Described as a potential "PyTorch moment" for agentic AI, it replaces manual tuning with a systematic evolution process, using benchmarks and automated mutations to enhance agent skills, memory, and performance. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Salesforce VoiceAgentRAG Drastically Reduces Voice AI Latency**
Salesforce AI Research has developed VoiceAgentRAG, a dual-agent memory router that dramatically speeds up Retrieval-Augmented Generation for voice assistants. The system reportedly cuts retrieval latency by 316 times, enabling voice agents to respond within the 200ms window required for natural-feeling conversation. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Ollama Leverages Apple's MLX to Speed Up Local LLMs on Macs**
The Ollama platform, used for running large language models locally, has been updated to use Apple's MLX framework. This integration allows for significantly faster performance and more efficient memory usage when running AI models on Mac computers equipped with Apple silicon, making local AI more practical for Apple users. (Source: The New Stack)

**Google Introduces AppFunctions to Connect AI Agents with Android Apps**
As part of a push to make Android an "agent-first" operating system, Google has unveiled AppFunctions in an early beta. This new feature provides a framework for apps to expose their functionalities as building blocks that AI agents and assistants can use to complete complex, multi-app tasks on behalf of the user. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### Security, Governance, and Public Perception

**LiteLLM Open-Source Project Compromised in Supply Chain Attack**
The popular open-source AI gateway LiteLLM was the target of a supply chain attack on the PyPI repository, with a compromised version being downloaded over 40,000 times. The malicious payload was designed to steal sensitive information, and the attack directly led to a security breach at AI recruiting startup Mercor, which used the tool. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Polls Reveal Growing AI Adoption but Declining Trust**
A new Quinnipiac University poll indicates that while more Americans are adopting AI tools, their trust in the results is decreasing. The poll highlights widespread public concern about transparency, regulation, and the societal impact of AI. A separate finding from the same poll revealed that 15% of Americans would be willing to have an AI program as their direct supervisor. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**IRS Tests Palantir AI for Identifying Audit Targets**
Documents reveal the US Internal Revenue Service (IRS) is testing a tool from data analytics firm Palantir to help identify high-value audit and investigation targets. The system is designed to sift through the agency's complex legacy systems to surface potential fraud, particularly related to clean energy credits. (Source: Wired)

**Anthropic Users Report Faster Than Normal Usage Limits on Claude**
Users of Anthropic's Claude models, particularly its coding assistant, have reported hitting their message and usage limits much faster than usual. The issue, which Anthropic has acknowledged, has caused frustration among developers who rely on the service for their workflows, raising questions about the model's verbosity or changes in how usage is calculated. (Source: The New Stack)

**Critiques Mount Over Reliability of LLM Benchmarks and Outputs**
The reliability of AI is under scrutiny, with one article labeling common LLM benchmarks as "junk science," citing an Oxford review that found 84% of benchmarks lack basic statistical testing. In a practical test, a WIRED journalist found that asking ChatGPT for product recommendations resulted in completely fabricated answers, highlighting the persistent issue of model hallucination. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

### AI Research and Scientific Applications

**MIT Researchers Use AI to Uncover Atomic Defects in Materials**
A new AI model developed at MIT can identify and measure atomic-level defects in materials. This is significant because these defects can be strategically leveraged to improve a material's properties, such as mechanical strength, heat transfer, and energy-conversion efficiency, opening new avenues in materials science. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**Mantis Biotech Creates 'Digital Twins' of Humans for Medical Research**
Startup Mantis Biotech is using AI to create synthetic datasets that serve as "digital twins" of the human body, representing anatomy, physiology, and behavior. By generating realistic but artificial data, the company aims to solve the problem of data availability in medical research, allowing for more robust model training without compromising patient privacy. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**New AI Tool Helps Visualize 3D-Printed Objects to Reduce Waste**
MIT researchers have created VisiPrint, a preview tool that helps makers visualize the final appearance of 3D-printed objects before fabrication. By quickly generating aesthetically accurate previews, the system allows users to catch errors and make adjustments early, which could make the prototyping process faster and less wasteful. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)
