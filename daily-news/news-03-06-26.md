## AI News Digest

### Major Model &amp; Product Releases

**OpenAI Launches GPT-5.4 Frontier Model**
OpenAI has released GPT-5.4, its newest and most capable model, designed for professional work. It features state-of-the-art performance in coding, computer use, and tool search, along with a one-million-token context window, significantly expanding its capacity for complex tasks. The model is available in "Pro" and "Thinking" versions, marking a major step forward in AI capabilities. (Source: OpenAI News, TechCrunch)

**OpenAI Integrates ChatGPT into Microsoft Excel**
Alongside its new model, OpenAI has introduced ChatGPT for Excel, powered by GPT-5.4. This integration aims to accelerate financial modeling, research, and analysis directly within spreadsheets. The move signifies a major push to embed advanced AI into core enterprise workflows, particularly in regulated environments like finance. (Source: OpenAI News)

**Google Expands Gemini Features with Canvas and Flash-Lite**
Google has rolled out "Canvas in AI Mode" in Google Search to all users in the U.S., allowing them to use AI to draft documents, create plans, and build interactive tools. The company also announced Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, a new model variant built for delivering "intelligence at scale," continuing the rapid expansion of its Gemini ecosystem. (Source: AI, TechCrunch)

**YuanLab AI Releases 1-Trillion Parameter Yuan 3.0 Ultra**
YuanLab AI has open-sourced Yuan 3.0 Ultra, a flagship multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) foundation model. While it has one trillion total parameters, it only activates 68.8 billion at a time, a design intended to deliver state-of-the-art performance with significantly improved pre-training efficiency and lower inference costs. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Luma Unveils Creative AI Agents with 'Unified Intelligence' Models**
Creative AI company Luma has launched Luma Agents, a new class of agents powered by its proprietary "Unified Intelligence" models. These agents are designed to coordinate multiple AI systems to generate end-to-end creative projects, spanning text, images, video, and audio, showcasing a move toward more holistic, multi-modal AI creation. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Liquid AI Releases LocalCowork for Privacy-First Agent Workflows**
Liquid AI has released LocalCowork, an open-source desktop application, and LFM2-24B-A2B, a model optimized for on-device tool use. The system enables complex agentic workflows to run entirely locally, eliminating API calls and data egress, which is critical for enterprises with high privacy and security requirements. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### Geopolitics, Military &amp; Regulation

**Pentagon Labels Anthropic a "Supply-Chain Risk" Amidst Contract Dispute**
The U.S. Department of Defense has officially designated AI firm Anthropic a "supply-chain risk," a first for an American company, after a reported $200 million contract broke down. The dispute allegedly arose from the military's demand for unrestricted access to Anthropic's AI, a condition the safety-focused company refused. Anthropic's CEO plans to challenge the label, even as reports indicate the military continues to use its Claude model for targeting in Iran. (Source: TechCrunch, Wired)

**Anthropic CEO Accuses OpenAI of "Lies" Over Military Deal**
In the fallout from its own Pentagon deal collapse, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei reportedly accused OpenAI of "straight up lies" regarding its messaging around its own military contracts. The conflict highlights the intense competition and differing ethical stances among leading AI labs as they engage with defense agencies. (Source: TechCrunch)

**US Considers Sweeping New Chip Export Controls**
The U.S. government is reportedly drafting a proposal for new, far-reaching export controls on advanced semiconductor chips. The new rules would potentially give the government oversight of every chip export sale, regardless of the destination country, in a significant escalation of efforts to control the global AI hardware supply chain. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Pentagon Tested OpenAI Models Before Military Ban Was Lifted**
A report alleges that the U.S. Department of Defense was experimenting with OpenAI's models via Microsoft's Azure cloud services even before OpenAI publicly removed its ban on military applications. This suggests that military exploration of commercial AI models was occurring through third-party cloud providers, bypassing direct prohibitions. (Source: Wired)

### Cloud, Infrastructure &amp; Hardware

**OpenAI Secures Massive $110B Multi-Cloud Deal with AWS**
OpenAI has reportedly expanded its partnership with Amazon Web Services in a deal valued at $110 billion, making AWS the exclusive third-party distributor for its "Frontier" stateful agent platform. The architecture will be split, with Microsoft Azure retaining exclusivity for stateless API services while AWS hosts the stateful runtime environments on its Bedrock platform. (Source: InfoQ)

**Big Tech Signs White House Data Center Power Pledge**
Leading tech and AI companies signed a pledge at the White House to invest in new, clean power generation to support their energy-intensive data centers. However, critics have pointed out that the pledge lacks enforcement mechanisms and may serve more as a public relations move than a substantive commitment to addressing AI's growing energy consumption. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica, Wired)

**Nvidia Signals Pullback from Investing in AI Labs**
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang announced that his company's recent investments in major AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic will likely be its last. This signals a strategic shift for the chipmaker, moving away from direct financial stakes in the companies that are also its largest customers. (Source: TechCrunch)

**RISC-V Architecture Poised to Challenge Arm and x86 in AI**
The open-standard RISC-V chip architecture is rapidly maturing and is now seen as a viable challenger to the dominance of Arm and x86, especially for AI workloads. Its customizability and royalty-free model are attracting developers looking to build specialized, high-performance processors for AI, potentially reshaping the semiconductor landscape. (Source: The New Stack)

### AI in Business &amp; Finance

**JPMorgan's Tech Spending to Near $20B, Driven by AI Expansion**
JPMorgan Chase is significantly increasing its investment in artificial intelligence, with the bank's total technology budget projected to approach $19.8 billion by 2026. This massive spending plan illustrates how major financial institutions are moving AI from experimental pilots into core business operations and systems. (Source: AI News)

**Rowspace Launches with $50M to Build AI for Private Equity**
A new startup, Rowspace, has launched with $50 million in funding from firms including Sequoia and Emergence to create AI specifically for the private equity industry. The platform aims to unify and analyze decades of scattered data, such as deal memos and portfolio data, to help scale and improve investment judgment. (Source: AI News)

**Dyna.Ai Raises Eight-Figure Series A for Financial Services Agents**
Singapore-based Dyna.Ai, an AI-as-a-Service company, has closed an eight-figure Series A funding round. The investment is aimed at helping the financial services industry move beyond small-scale AI pilot projects to deploy robust, production-grade agentic AI systems. (Source: AI News)

**AI Procurement Startup Lio Raises $30M from Andreessen Horowitz**
Lio, a startup using AI to automate enterprise procurement, announced a $30 million Series A round led by Andreessen Horowitz. The company's platform aims to streamline the complex and often manual processes involved in corporate purchasing and vendor management. (Source: TechCrunch)

**AI Customer Support Startup Decagon Reaches $4.5B Valuation**
Decagon, an AI-powered customer support startup, completed its first tender offer, establishing a new valuation of $4.5 billion. The rapid growth highlights strong investor confidence in AI solutions that automate and enhance customer service operations for large enterprises. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Research Suggests Autonomous AI Agents Would Prefer Bitcoin**
A non-partisan study from the Bitcoin Policy Institute evaluated how frontier AI models would behave as independent economic actors. The research concluded that due to its properties of decentralization, scarcity, and verifiability, autonomous AI agents would logically prefer to use Bitcoin for digital wealth storage and transactions. (Source: AI News)

### Developer Tools &amp; Open Source Frameworks

**OpenAI Releases 'Symphony' Agentic Framework**
OpenAI has open-sourced Symphony, a framework for orchestrating autonomous AI coding agents. Built using Elixir, it provides a structured system for automating software development tasks by connecting LLM-based agents directly to issue trackers, enabling them to manage and execute implementation runs. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Releases 'gws' CLI for Workspace APIs**
Google AI has launched an open-source command-line interface (CLI) tool named 'gws' to provide a unified interface for Google Workspace APIs like Drive, Gmail, and Sheets. The tool is designed to be used by both human developers and AI agents, simplifying interactions with Google's productivity suite. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Cursor Rolls Out 'Automations' for Agentic Coding**
The AI-native code editor Cursor is introducing a new system called Automations, which allows developers to trigger AI agents automatically within their coding environment. These triggers can be based on events like a new code commit, a Slack message, or a simple timer, further embedding agentic capabilities into the development lifecycle. (Source: TechCrunch)

**LangWatch Open Sources Evaluation Layer for AI Agents**
To address the non-determinism of LLM-based systems, LangWatch has open-sourced a platform for end-to-end tracing, simulation, and systematic testing of AI agents. The tool provides a standardized evaluation layer to help developers build more reliable and predictable autonomous systems. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Cloudflare Debuts Tools to Guide AI Crawlers**
Cloudflare has introduced "Markdown for Agents," a feature allowing AI crawlers to request a simplified Markdown version of a web page for easier processing. It was released alongside a "Content Signals" proposal, a mechanism for publishers to declare whether their content can be used for AI training, indexing, or inference. (Source: InfoQ)

### Robotics &amp; Physical AI

**Google Integrates Industrial Robotics Unit 'Intrinsic' into Core Business**
Alphabet's industrial robotics software company, Intrinsic, has been officially folded into Google. The move signals a major strategic bet on physical AI, with Intrinsic set to work closely with Google DeepMind and leverage Gemini models to make industrial robots more intelligent and accessible. (Source: AI News)

**New 'MEM' System Gives Robots 15-Minute Memory**
Researchers from Physical Intelligence, Stanford, and MIT have unveiled MEM, a multi-scale memory system for robots. The system provides Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with up to 15 minutes of context, enabling them to perform complex, long-horizon tasks like cleaning a kitchen that were previously intractable due to memory limitations. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Hugging Face Details Path to Robotics on Embedded Platforms**
A technical blog from Hugging Face outlined the process of bringing advanced robotics AI to small, embedded platforms. The work covers the full pipeline, including recording custom datasets, fine-tuning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, and performing on-device optimizations for efficient real-world deployment. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

### AI Safety, Ethics &amp; Privacy

**Meta Sued Over Smart Glasses Privacy After Reviewers See Sensitive Footage**
Meta is facing a lawsuit over its AI-powered smart glasses after an investigation revealed that human subcontractors were reviewing highly sensitive footage captured by users, including nudity and sexual activity. The lawsuit alleges this practice contradicts Meta's marketing, which promised user privacy and control over shared footage. (Source: TechCrunch)

**LLMs Can Reliably Unmask Pseudonymous Online Users, Study Finds**
New research demonstrates that large language models can analyze the writing styles of pseudonymous users across different platforms to de-anonymize them with surprising accuracy. This capability poses a significant threat to online privacy, potentially rendering pseudonymity an ineffective protection method. (Source: Ars Technica)

**Apple Music to Add 'Transparency Tags' for AI-Generated Music**
Apple Music will reportedly introduce "Transparency Tags" to identify music created with AI. However, the system is opt-in, meaning labels and distributors must choose to apply the tag, which raises questions about its potential effectiveness in providing comprehensive transparency to listeners. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Grammarly Offers AI Writing Feedback in the Style of Famous Authors**
The company Grammarly, recently rebranded as Superhuman, is offering a new AI feature that provides writing feedback based on the styles of famous authors, both living and dead. The tool has sparked ethical and copyright concerns, as it uses the authors' work without their permission. (Source: Wired)

**OpenAI Research Finds Reasoning Models Are Hard to Control**
In a new safety paper, OpenAI introduced a technique called CoT-Control and found that reasoning models struggle to deliberately control their own chains of thought. The company frames this as a positive for safety, as the difficulty in hiding reasoning steps makes the models more monitorable and auditable. (Source: OpenAI News)

## CRICKET News Digest

### Men's T20 World Cup Finals Set

**India Edge England in Semi-Final Thriller**
India secured a place in the T20 World Cup final by defeating England by just seven runs in a high-scoring classic. Sanju Samson's crucial 89 propelled India to a massive total, which proved just enough despite a stunning 105 from England's Jacob Bethell. Jasprit Bumrah's exceptional death bowling was widely praised as the deciding factor in the tense finish. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

**Allen's Century Powers New Zealand into Final**
New Zealand demolished South Africa by nine wickets to book their spot in the final against India. Finn Allen was the star, smashing an unbeaten century to chase down the target with ease. The dominant performance ensures New Zealand will play in their second Men's T20 World Cup final. (Source: BBC Sport)

**Bumrah Hailed as Decisive 'Superpower'**
Following his match-winning spell against England, Jasprit Bumrah received high praise from teammates and opponents. Sanju Samson stated he "would not be standing here if not for Bumrah," while former South Africa captain Faf du Plessis described having the pacer in the team as being "like having a genie." (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

### Australian Domestic Season Heats Up

**WA Dominates Queensland in Shield Clash**
Western Australia is in a commanding position against Queensland, jeopardizing the latter's Sheffield Shield final hopes. Debutant Kyle Esterhuysen's 4-37 helped dismiss Queensland for 173, before Sam Fanning's unbeaten 82 guided WA to a substantial first-innings lead. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

**Sangha Century Puts South Australia in Control**
A century from Jason Sangha (115) has given South Australia a significant advantage over New South Wales. Fifties from Alex Carey (58) and Henry Scott (61) also contributed as South Australia posted a formidable first-innings total of 373. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

**Labuschagne Named One-Day Player of the Year**
Marnus Labuschagne has been awarded the Marsh One-Day Cup Player of the Year for his outstanding form. He secured the award after a prolific season that saw him score four centuries in just six innings for Queensland. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)

Brendon McCullum has stated he "would love to carry on" as England's head coach following the team's semi-final exit from the T20 World Cup. Despite a challenging winter, McCullum reiterated his commitment to the role and the team's identity. (Source: BBC Sport)

**Hampshire Sign Seales to Replace Neser**
Hampshire has signed West Indies fast bowler Jayden Seales for the opening part of the County Championship season. Seales comes in as a replacement for Australian Michael Neser, whose deal was cancelled after he earned a central contract with Cricket Australia. (Source: BBC Sport)

New Zealand crushed Zimbabwe by a record 180 runs in their ODI clash. The victory was built on Brooke Halliday's magnificent maiden century (157 not out) and a dominant bowling display from sisters Amelia and Jess Kerr, who took seven wickets between them. (Source: ESPN Cricinfo.com)
